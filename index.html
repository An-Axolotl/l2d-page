Language to Design (L2D): Towards Self Evolving Robots

Anonymous authors · paper under double-blind review
🎬 Task Videos 🔁 Transfer
From a sentence and a stock robot to a co-designed body, a motion that performs the task, and manufacturable parts.

Abstract

Designing a robot requires expertise in both complex mechanics and control. Adapting a robot to a task relies typically on slow cycles of guessing, prototyping and testing to find where design and behavior align. This raises a fundamental question: can robot design be made as simple as describing what we want a robot to do? We present Language to Design (L2D), a co-design framework that turns a prompt and an existing robot into an optimized body, a motion that performs the task, and manufacturable parts, without tuning or training per task or per robot. The framework supports 1) language proposals grounded in measured physics, where language reflection decides what to change and physics measured in simulation decides how much, 2) a continuous design space, spanning link kinematics, motors, added joints, and continuous morphology designs for contact points such as feet and fingers, and 3) real-world fabrication, where stress-based topology optimization turns added parts into light, manufacturable components deployed zero shot on hardware. We demonstrate L2D by co-designing quadrupeds, arms, grippers, dexterous hands, and humanoids across eleven tasks on nine robots, and deploy a printed design zero shot on a real quadruped for a climbing task.

Co-Designed Robots in Action

Every clip below is a design L2D found for the task, replayed from its judged MuJoCo episode and rendered in Blender; orange marks the parts the design added or reshaped, and the title states the change. Use vs. stock to see the same start flown by the unmodified robot side by side.

Main tasks

Nine tasks across quadrupeds, arms, grippers, a humanoid and a hand, each found by a normal L2D search with its own design space.

Hurdle Pass

UNITREE GO2 · QUADRUPED
“Hurdle over the wall in front of you: get over it and past it.”

A Go2 with rear thighs 6 cm longer and stronger rear knees clears a 0.42 m board cleanly, never touching it (foot apex 0.43 m), and lands well past it.

Toss Pass

UR5e + ROBOTIQ 2F-85 · ARM
“Throw the ball as far as you can.”

A physics-guided edit of the arm's link geometry, a forearm stretched 2.4× to 0.96 m on a lighter upper arm, throws the ball 33 m on this start (the stock arm: 1.3 m). Ground markers every 5 m.

Bin pick Pass

FRANKA FR3 + GENERATED GRIPPER
“Pick the can up out of the bin … put it down inside the green bin.”

A generated four-finger gripper with curved claw fingers moves the can to the green bin and leaves it upright in under 3 s, on 6 of 6 held-out starts.

Free throw Pass

UNITREE G1 + HANDS · HUMANOID
“Shoot it into the hoop without stepping over the line.”

A G1 with softer right shoulder and elbow actuators sinks the shot through the hoop on 6 of 6 held-out starts with its fine-tuned controller.

Pen spin Hardest task

SHARPA HAND · DEXTEROUS HAND
“Spin it one full turn without dropping it, ending steady.”

The hardest task in the suite. A hand with wider thumb and knuckle travel and softer side-to-side knuckles turns the pen a full turn on this held-out start without dropping it; on other starts it makes partial turns.

Box climb Pass

UNITREE GO2 + PRINTED PARTS · QUADRUPED
“Climb up onto the box in front of you (0.45 m high) and stand on it.”

Printed tread feet over the stock calves let the Go2 climb onto a 0.45 m box and stand on all four feet (5 of 6 starts); the stock Go2 does not. This is the design we fabricate for hardware.

Soccer Goal

UNITREE ALIENGO · QUADRUPED
“Shoot this soccer ball into the top left hand corner of the net … without crossing over the line.”

An Aliengo with cup-shaped printed feet, heavier calves and a lighter trunk shoots into the top-left corner without crossing the line, scoring on 5 of 6 starts; the stock Aliengo scores on none.

Standing long jump Pass

UNITREE GO2 · QUADRUPED
“Jump as far forward as you can, landing on your feet.”

Measured like a human long jump, from where the front feet leave to where the rear-most foot lands. A Go2 with telescoping shins, pointed-toe feet and a 3 kg lighter trunk jumps 0.43 m and counts on 5 of 6 starts; the stock Go2 counts on none.

Curling Pass

UR5e + ROBOTIQ 2F-85 · ARM
“Slide the stone down the ice and let go of it before the red line.”

A UR5e with an extra wrist twist joint, a shorter upper arm and a longer forearm slides the stone to rest in the house, on the ice. The stock UR5e’s stone leaves the ice.

Additional tasks

Three more tasks, run with the loop unchanged.

Hammer Pass

FRANKA PANDA + UMI GRIPPER · GRIPPER ONLY
“Use the hammer to drive the nail into the wooden board.”

Only the gripper may change: a right finger 9 cm longer with a firmer grip holds the hammer and drives the nail in cleanly—only the hammer touches the nail. Shown with a smooth, jitter-free controller.

Curling Pass

KINOVA GEN3 + 2F-85 · ARM
“Slide the stone down the ice and let go of it before the red line.”

An arm with a 3.4 cm shorter upper arm, heavier elbow and damped wrist releases the stone before the line and it comes to rest in the house. Shown with a smooth, jitter-free controller that passes the judge without the arm touching the ice.

Rocky slope Pass

ANYMAL C · QUADRUPED
“Sprint up the rocky slope to the yellow mark at the top.”

An ANYmal C with hips raised up to 10 cm and a 4 kg lighter trunk scrambles up through the loose rocks with only its feet touching the stones. The stock robot also passes on this start, so this task shows generality rather than a gain.

Transfer Between Tasks

Designs found on one task seed the first round of a new run on a related task, on the same robot.

Pit leap → long jump Pass

UNITREE GO2 · QUADRUPED

A pit-leap design, reworked across the whole body (shorter, lighter torso; telescoping shins; stronger motors), jumps up to 0.61 m on 6 of 6 starts.

Long jump → pit leap Pass

UNITREE GO2 · QUADRUPED

Transfer tests whether a robot designed for one task carries over to a similar one. A long-jump Go2, given one edit (2.25× feet, retuned calves), leaps the lava pit; the stock Go2 does not.

Standing long jump Pass

UNITREE GO2 · QUADRUPED

Measured like a human long jump, front-feet takeoff to rear-foot landing. A Go2 with telescoping shins and a lighter trunk jumps 0.43 m (5 of 6 starts); the stock Go2 never counts.

Toss → curling Pass

UR5e + ROBOTIQ 2F-85 · ARM

Starting from toss designs, the loop adds a second shoulder joint and shortens the upper arm; the stone comes to rest in the house. The stock UR5e also makes this shot with the same control.

Transfer within one robot (Go2 long jump to pit leap; UR5e toss to curling) and the box climb with printed feet.
