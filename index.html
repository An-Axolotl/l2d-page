Language To Design (L2D): Towards Self Evolving Robots

Anonymous authors · paper under double-blind review
🎬 Task Videos 🤖 Real World 🔁 Transfer
From a sentence and a stock robot to a co-designed body, a motion that performs the task, and manufacturable parts.

Abstract

Designing a robot requires expertise in both complex mechanics and control, and relies on slow cycles of guessing, prototyping, and testing to find where design and behavior align. This raises a fundamental question: can robot design be made as simple as describing what we want a robot to do? We present Language to Design (L2D), a training-free co-design framework that turns a prompt and a starting robot into an optimized design and controller, along with manufacturable parts. L2D introduces 1) prompt-to-design proposals grounded in measured physics, where language decides what to change and simulation decides how much; 2) a general co-design space across arms, grippers, hands, quadrupeds, and humanoids, spanning kinematics, motors, added joints, and continuous contact shapes; and 3) a training-free path from prompt to fabrication, in which added parts are optimized under simulated loads, 3D printed, and deployed on hardware. We demonstrate L2D on 15 tasks across five robot families, with two tasks on real hardware.
